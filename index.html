The idea

The energy isn't in the decision. It's in everything after it.

Every sensor event that gets forwarded pays for a whole chain of downstream work — on-device processing, compression, a radio uplink, the cloud. Reject it early, near the sensor, and almost none of that energy is ever spent.

For a rejected event, the on-device and cloud tiers never wake — you pay the $ tier and skip the $$ and $$$. Cloud cost is recurring, per device, forever.  $ / energy figures are relative magnitudes, illustrating the escalation.

save > cost Adding intelligence spends its own silicon and energy, so the design principle is energy ROI: the early decision must prevent far more downstream energy than it costs. When a tiny check on the common case skips the whole chain, its value dwarfs "low-energy inference."
What you're buying

A drop-in decision fabric — always-on, integrated into your silicon.

Licensed as an IP core, integrated into your floorplan like any other block. It takes in any sensor stream, carries a library of models in flash, and runs them at a fraction of the energy a conventional neural accelerator spends — because it works a fundamentally different way.

It integrates cleanly because its spec sheet asks almost nothing of the chip around it:

01No multiplier arrays. Zero DSP blocks — the most power- and area-hungry part of a neural engine is simply gone.
02No external memory. Models stream from cheap serial flash — no DRAM, no host processor to babysit it.
03Deterministic & bit-exact. A fixed cycle count per compiled model — easy to close timing and verify.
04Tiny & field-updatable. A small logic footprint; new models load from flash after the chip ships.

It drops into an SoC, MCU, or sensor without disrupting the design — and turns that chip into a system that decides at the sensor.

More than a gate — a fabric

Real products aren't one model — they're a chain of decisions.

Detect, then identify, then act. One Spot Vision fabric runs the whole chain — holding a library of models in flash that it switches between instantly. Waking another processor is just the simplest thing it can do.

One silicon design becomes a family of products, and a shipped chip gets smarter over its life — new models pushed to flash, no re-spin, no recall.

GateA cheap always-on model wakes a heavier one only when something happens — the common case costs almost nothing.
CascadeIncreasingly capable models handle increasingly hard cases — accuracy where it's needed, battery everywhere else.
PipelineDetect → classify → verify, all inside the one fabric, with no host to orchestrate it.

That's why it's a fabric, not just a wake pin — one licensed block that sells into many end products and keeps earning as models improve.
